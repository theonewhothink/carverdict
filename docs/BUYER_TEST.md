# Test the first MotorJury buying brief

Status: prepared, not conducted. No customers or mechanics have reviewed this release.

## Who to invite

Ten US buyers who bought a used car recently or expect to buy one soon. Include first-time and experienced buyers. Ideally they are considering a 2019–2020 RAV4. Ask them to use a real candidate without entering a VIN, identity, seller documents or payment details. Otherwise clearly label the exercise as an example rather than pretend it was their purchase.

Invitation draft — send only through a channel the publisher authorises:

> We are testing a free used-car buying brief. We want to see whether it helps you prepare for a viewing and compare costs. It takes about 20 minutes. You do not need an account or payment details. We are testing the website, not you; criticism helps. Would you like to take part?

Consent script:

> We will record task completion, time, misunderstandings and your feedback under a participant code. Do not share your VIN, seller paperwork or personal financial details. Participation is voluntary, and you can stop at any time. We will not record audio or video unless you separately agree.

## Tasks — read without explaining the interface

1. Open the brief on your phone. Identify the candidate's year and powertrain. Explain what changes when the powertrain is unknown.
2. Tell us the main documented concern and what you would do before a deposit. Does a listed campaign establish that your car is affected or repaired?
3. Prepare a checklist you could use at the viewing. Save or download it without an account.
4. Compare two candidates using the same mileage, fuel price and holding period. First leave insurance or resale blank. Explain what the result can and cannot say. Then complete a clearly labelled example.
5. Share the brief. Tell us whether the link includes your budget or checked items.
6. Use the resource you normally use for this task. Which would you choose, for what specific reason, and what is missing?

Do not coach. Record help separately from independent completion. If a dangerous misunderstanding appears, record it and correct it after the task. Avoid leading questions such as “Was the checklist useful?”

## Decision

Internal product gate: at least 8/10 complete identification → documented concern → next check → usable saved brief without help; at least 6/10 prefer it for a specific reason; no dangerous misunderstanding of recalls, condition or costs. These are our design targets, not Google's rules or a representative survey.

If the gate fails, repair the causes and retest with new participants before expanding the collection. Obtain a real qualified mechanic's review of consequential inspection advice before claiming specialist review. Publish the observed findings and limitations, including unfavourable feedback. Never substitute simulated users for this test.
