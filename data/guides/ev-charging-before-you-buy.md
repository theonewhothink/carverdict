The car in the advert has enough range for your usual day. That is encouraging. Now picture arriving home late on a Tuesday: where will you park, how will you charge and what happens if that spot is unavailable?

The brochure cannot answer those questions for your street. A simple plan based on a real week can tell you more about whether an EV fits your routine than another comparison of headline range figures.

## Start where the car will spend the night

Write down your actual parking arrangement. If it is shared or rented, ask the person responsible what permission and installation process applies. Do not assume that a nearby socket means an approved charging installation.

The US Department of Energy's home-charging guidance discusses household electrical capacity, equipment and installation requirements. Ask a qualified installer about your own property and local requirements. This page is a planning aid, not electrical installation advice. US voltage and charging-level descriptions should not be carried into another country's installation plan.

Your useful next item is a property-specific answer and quote, rather than a guessed installation price in a comparison spreadsheet.

## Map the week, then find the backup

List your ordinary journeys and when the car is parked. Include the inconvenient day as well as the easy one. A school run, an unexpected evening journey or shared access to a charger may change the plan.

Look up a realistic alternative near a place you already visit. Confirm access, current payment terms and the connector compatibility for the exact car, charger and any manufacturer-approved adapter. A station marker on a map does not establish every detail of a successful charging stop.

DOE explains that charging time depends on the vehicle, its battery state and the charging equipment. A station's advertised power is therefore not a promise that your candidate will accept that power throughout the session.

## Keep three costs on separate lines

Record an installation quote if you need one. Record the relevant home electricity tariff. Then record the public-charging terms you would actually use. Leave an unanswered line blank while you investigate it; zero suggests you have confirmed that there is no cost.

Do not compare electricity priced per kilowatt-hour with fuel priced per litre as if the numbers mean the same thing. Both need a consumption figure, a stated distance and consistent units. The arithmetic should follow the evidence you collected, not produce a confident saving from missing inputs.

## Write a plan you could explain to someone else

Try this sentence: “On a usual week I can charge here, during these hours, under these terms. If that fails, my next option is here.” Any word you cannot fill in is a useful question before a deposit.

Enjoy exploring the cars in [the catalogue](/cars/). For the buying decision, keep the charging plan attached to the actual vehicle and your own circumstances.
