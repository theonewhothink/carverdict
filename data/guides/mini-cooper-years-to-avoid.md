The [Mini Cooper](/cars/mini/cooper/) has no year to avoid on the federal record, and that is the first thing to say about it. Every one of its six scored years is a BUY: the worst, [2013](/cars/mini/cooper/2013/), scores 80 on 143 complaints, and the best, [2014](/cars/mini/cooper/2014/), scores 95 on 66 complaints with no recall campaign at all. The second thing to say is that the record stops. The 2016 to 2024 cars have no complaints on file and are DATA PENDING, so a buyer looking at anything newer than a 2015 gets no verdict from this site, and no reassurance either. All 541 complaints on file belong to the 2011 to 2015 cars, and this guide is about those five years.

## 2011 to 2013: an electrical pattern and one late recall

The 2011 car is the quietest of the first three: 92 complaints, 6.1 per year on road, a score of 91. The 2012 car rises to 134 complaints and 9.6 per year and scores 84; the 2013 car rises again to 143 and 11.0 per year and scores 80. The component pattern is the same in each year, and it is electrical. Electrical System is the top category with 41 complaints on the 2011 car, 56 on the 2012 and 57 on the 2013, and Air Bags is second or third at 19, 25 and 35. Engine is 13 on the 2011 car and 34 on the 2012, but drops out of the 2013 car's top five, where Fuel/Propulsion System at 15 and Seat Belts at 12 appear instead. Exterior Lighting shows up at 9 and 10 on the 2011 and 2012 cars.

Each of these three years carries exactly one recall, and it is the same one: 23V337000, filed by BMW of North America on 11/05/2023, a safety-critical Electrical System campaign covering the 2007 to 2013 two-door Hardtop and the 2008 to 2014 Clubman, but only cars sold or ever registered in a list of states that begins with Alaska and Colorado. It arrived a decade after the last of the affected cars was built, and it is the only campaign on record for the 2011 to 2013 cars. That the top complaint category in all three years is the one the recall eventually addressed is the closest the record comes to naming a cause; the sheet does not say what the electrical fault is, and I will not guess.

## 2014: a redesigned car and a flat record

The 2014 model year brought a redesigned Mini, and the record changes with it. The EPA combined figure moves from 30 to 29 miles per gallon on premium fuel, and the complaint pattern flattens: 66 complaints in total, 5.5 per year on road, with Electrical System, Engine and Power Train tied at 16 each, Structure at 8 and Fuel/Propulsion System at 4. No category dominates, and there is no recall campaign on the 2014 car. That combination, a low count spread thinly across categories with nothing the manufacturer had to fix, is what a 95 looks like on this site, and the 2014 is the used Mini to look for. Its evidence label is medium, the same as every other scored year from 2011 to 2015, so the score is not resting on a handful of reports.

## 2015: the pattern moves to the engine

The 2015 car keeps the clean recall sheet, no campaigns, but its complaints climb to 106, 9.6 per year on road, and the score drops to 86. The mix is different from both the 2011 to 2013 cars and the 2014. Engine is top at 28, Fuel/Propulsion System second at 22, then Electrical System and Power Train at 18 each and Service Brakes at 14. The electrical dominance of the earlier cars is gone; what replaced it is a spread of engine and fuel-system reports that no recall corroborates. The record therefore shows a symptom pattern, not a documented fault, and a buyer should treat the 2015 as a good car whose engine history deserves more attention than a 2014's. The 2015 car's 9.6 complaints per year on road is the same rate as the 2012 car's, and the 2012 scores 84; the 2015 scores higher because its record has no recall in it.

## Nine years without a record, and the 2025 car

From 2016 to 2024 the Mini Cooper has zero complaints on file for every model year and a verdict of DATA PENDING with evidence "none". The EPA figures show the cars exist and kept improving, 30 miles per gallon combined through 2019, 31 in 2020 and 2021, 32 from 2022 to 2024, but there is nothing on the complaint or recall side to score. This is not the same as a clean record; it is an absent one, and the rule on this site is that no conclusion can be drawn from it. A 2018 Mini may be a fine car. The federal record cannot tell you so.

The 2025 car scores 90 on low evidence, again with no complaints, and one safety-critical recall: 25V616000 (17/09/2025), for a torsion bar in both front seat belt retractors that may have been damaged during production. That recall and the score are all the record contains for it.

One point applies to every year on the sheet: the EPA rates each of them on premium fuel, from 2011 through 2025.

## What to check before you buy

- On a 2011 to 2013 car, run the VIN through the NHTSA recall lookup for 23V337000. It is a regional campaign, so a car that spent its life outside the listed states may not be covered even though the same component is the top complaint in all three years. Ask where the car was first sold and registered.
- On the same cars, test every electrical function on the test drive and look at the service history for repeat electrical repairs, because Electrical System is 41, 56 and 57 complaints in these three years and nothing else comes close. Air Bags is the second pattern, 19 to 35 complaints a year: make sure no restraint warning is showing and ask whether any air bag work has been done.
- On a 2015 car, read the engine and fuel-system service history closely and drive it from cold. Engine at 28 and Fuel/Propulsion System at 22 are the top two categories and there is no recall behind them, so the paperwork is the only evidence you will get.
- On a 2014 car, there is nothing specific the record points to; a standard pre-purchase inspection is enough.
- On a 2016 to 2024 car, the record is empty and cannot help. Use a service history and an independent inspection as you would for any car with no data, and do not read the absence of complaints as a verdict.
- On a 2025 car, confirm 25V616000 has been completed.

## Summary

There is no Mini Cooper year to avoid on the record. The 2014 car, with 66 complaints, no recalls and a score of 95, is the one to buy; the 2015 at 86 and the 2011 at 91 are close behind. The 2012 and 2013 cars are BUY verdicts with a rising electrical pattern and one regional recall to check. The 2016 to 2024 cars have no record at all, and the 2025 car has one recall and low evidence.
