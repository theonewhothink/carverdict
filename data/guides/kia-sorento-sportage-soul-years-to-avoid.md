Kia's record on this site has two halves. The first, roughly 2011 to 2017, is dominated by the Theta II engine failures it shares with Hyundai and by a second, smaller engine problem of its own. The second, from 2018, is one of the cleanest runs of any mainstream brand. A buyer who knows where the line falls can buy a Kia with confidence; a buyer who does not can buy an engine.

## Sorento and Sportage: the Theta II years

The Sorento of 2011 to 2019 and the Sportage of 2011 to 2019 carry the 2.4-litre and 2.0-litre turbo Theta II engines that were recalled for connecting-rod bearing failure, and the pattern in their records matches the Optima's. The Sorento's worst years are 2011 (1,517 complaints, score 50) and 2016 (1,245, score 44); the years between are moderate, and from 2018 the scores climb — 84, 81, 91 for 2018 to 2020. The Sportage's counts are lower throughout because its volume is lower, but the shape is the same. The engine-recall guide on this site explains the campaign, the knock-sensor software and how to tell a replaced engine from an inspected one; everything in it applies here.

## Soul: the Gamma and Nu engines

The second-generation Soul (2014 to 2019) uses smaller engines — the 1.6-litre Gamma and the 2.0-litre Nu — that were not in the original Theta recall but were caught by a later one. On these engines a failing catalytic converter can break up and its debris damage the pistons, leading to a knock, loss of power and, in some cases, a fire; Kia recalled 2012 to 2016 Souls in 2019 and 2020 and extended the same knock-sensor software and engine warranty. The 2014 to 2016 Souls carry between 800 and 900 complaints a year and score around 40; the 2017 to 2019 cars score between 81 and 89. The third generation from 2020 has a small bump in its first year and then scores in the 80s and 90s.

## The good years

From 2018 onward Kia's record is strong across the range. The 2018 to 2020 Optima scores between 86 and 92; the 2020 Sorento 91; the 2020 to 2025 Sportage in the 80s; the Telluride, launched for 2020, has one of the better launch-year records on the site and scores well since. The fourth-generation Sorento from 2021 introduced a 2.5-litre turbo with a dual-clutch transmission that attracts some hesitation complaints, but the counts are low. The reasons are the same as Hyundai's: reworked engines, a corrected manufacturing process, and a fleet that has been through the recall campaigns.

## What to check

Any 2011 to 2019 Sorento or Sportage with a 2.0 turbo or 2.4-litre engine, and any 2014 to 2016 Soul: the recall status by VIN, the knock-sensor campaign, and whether the engine has been replaced. Listen to the engine cold; check the oil level; if the car is in reduced-power mode with a warning light, it is the knock sensor speaking. Any Kia: confirm the remaining engine warranty with a dealer by VIN, because the extended coverage is generous but its terms vary by campaign and ownership.

## Summary

Buy: Sorento 2018 onward, Sportage 2018 onward, Soul 2017 onward, Telluride any year. Avoid, or buy with a replaced engine documented: Sorento 2011 and 2016, Sportage 2011 to 2017, Soul 2014 to 2016.
