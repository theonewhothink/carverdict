A timeline makes car history look tidy. Put four dates in order and it can feel as though every project was one inevitable step towards the next. The dates become more interesting when you ask what actually happened at each one.

The visual above is a selected set of manufacturer-archive milestones. It is not a complete history of sports cars or a ranking of their engineering.

## Write the event beside the year

Lamborghini places the Miura's public debut in 1966. Ferrari's museum material identifies the F40 as a 1987 model designed by Pininfarina with extensive use of composites. Honda dates the original NSX's debut to 1990. McLaren describes the road-going F1 as realised in 1992.

Those descriptions are worth retaining. “Public debut,” “model introduction,” and a manufacturer's account of a project reaching completion are labels for particular events. They should not automatically become the build year of the vehicle in a photograph.

The sources are linked below so you can read how each manufacturer describes its own project. Their accounts establish what those organisations say about their history; our comparison between the projects is an editorial interpretation.

## Follow an idea through the gap

Choose one question to carry across the four entries. Where is the engine packaged? Which construction choice is the archive discussing? What does the body make you curious about?

Lamborghini's account discusses the Miura's transverse mid-mounted V12. Honda's NSX history discusses the all-aluminium monocoque. McLaren's F1 archive describes a carbon-fibre monocoque. These give you different starting points for looking closely, rather than a score to put above each photograph.

The [feature comparison](/guides/compare-car-design-briefs/) keeps those selected facts in a small table. It also explains why a photograph of the exterior cannot establish the material of every part underneath it.

## Keep later versions on their own line

A name can survive changes in construction, equipment, bodywork or purpose. When a source discusses a later derivative, keep its year and version in your notes instead of folding its details into the first introduction.

For example, McLaren's archive also discusses a racing derivative's 1995 Le Mans result. That is a different milestone from the 1992 road-car entry. The timeline here labels the road car and leaves that later event in the source discussion.

## Make a timeline you can check

For your own shortlist, keep four fields: the exact car or version, the event, the date stated by the source and a link to that source. Add the date you checked it. If the available photograph has an uncertain build year, say so beside the image.

You now have a useful memory aid rather than a claim that every picture or catalogue identifier has been independently authenticated. Start with the [reviewed design stories](/discover/) and follow the archive links for the idea that interests you.
