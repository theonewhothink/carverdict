The third-generation Ford Focus was, on paper, one of the best small cars of its decade — good to drive, well built, and available with a manual gearbox that enthusiasts liked. Most buyers took the automatic. The automatic was the PowerShift, a six-speed dual-clutch transmission with dry clutches, and it is the reason the 2012 Focus has 3,795 owner complaints on file with the federal regulator, the highest count of any single model year in this dataset.

## What a dry dual-clutch is, and why this one failed

A dual-clutch transmission is two manual gearboxes side by side, one holding the odd gears and one the even, each with its own clutch, so that the next gear is already selected when the current one releases. Volkswagen's version, the DSG, uses clutches bathed in oil. Ford's PowerShift used dry clutches, like a manual car, to save weight and cost. Dry clutches in stop-start traffic, controlled by software that had to guess how much the friction material had worn, shuddered on take-off, slipped between gears, engaged with a bang, and in many cars lost drive altogether. A seal on the input shaft leaked fluid onto the clutch faces and made it worse. The transmission control module failed on its own.

Ford issued software updates, replaced clutches and seals under an extended warranty that reached seven years or 100,000 miles, settled a class action in 2017 covering 2011 to 2016 Focus and Fiesta, and was the subject of a Detroit Free Press investigation into what the company knew and when. None of that changed the design. A 2012 Focus with a replaced clutch has a newer clutch of the same kind.

## The years

The 2011 Focus is the last of the previous generation, with a conventional four-speed automatic, and scores 94 on this site. The 2012 car scores 33; the 2013 car 48; the 2014 car 37. The 2015 to 2017 cars are better — the software was more mature, the seals had been changed in production, and the counts fall to around a thousand — but they still carry the same transmission, and the scores in the 60s reflect a car that is acceptable rather than good.

The Fiesta shares the transmission from 2011 to 2019 and the same shape of record. The 2019 Fiesta was the last year and the last PowerShift car sold in the United States.

## The way out

Every third-generation Focus was offered with a five-speed or six-speed manual gearbox, and the manual cars are absent from the transmission complaints entirely. The Focus ST and RS are manual-only. The Focus Electric has no transmission of this kind. A manual-gearbox Focus from 2012 to 2018 is a well-regarded small car with none of this history; if you can drive one, it is the answer. The 2018 Focus with the 1.0-litre three-cylinder was offered with a conventional six-speed torque-converter automatic, and that is the other route to a PowerShift-free Focus.

## What to check on an automatic car

Ask for the clutch and control-module replacement history under the extended warranty — a dealer can print it from the VIN. Drive it in traffic: the shudder on take-off, especially uphill, is the signature. Listen for a rattle at idle in neutral. If the check-engine light is on, do not assume it is minor. And price the car on the assumption that the clutch pack, at roughly 1,500 to 2,500 dollars fitted, will need to be done once more in your ownership.

## Summary

Buy: any manual-gearbox Focus, the 2011 automatic, the 2018 with the torque-converter automatic. Avoid: 2012 to 2014 PowerShift automatics. Acceptable at the right price: 2015 to 2017 PowerShift with documented clutch replacement.
