A model page lists a recall. The seller says the work was done. Your next step is to connect those two statements to the actual vehicle with dated records.

Use the workflow above as a way to organise that conversation. It does not determine whether a particular car is safe to drive.

## Begin with the right kind of result

A year/make/model search describes general records. A VIN search addresses a specific vehicle within the official service's coverage. [NHTSA explains the distinction and its lookup limits](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls).

Record the date and the kind of search you ran. A model campaign can identify a question worth asking without proving that every example of that model is affected. Keep that applicability question open until you have a vehicle-specific answer.

## Read the limits before interpreting a blank result

NHTSA's VIN tool does not show completed recalls. Its stated exclusions also include some newly announced campaigns, older recalls beyond its coverage window, non-safety manufacturer campaigns and international vehicles.

Those limits matter when a seller presents an empty result as a complete history. Ask which service was checked and when, then use the manufacturer or dealer to resolve the question that the result cannot answer.

## Ask for the record behind “done”

Request the campaign identifier, the vehicle identity used in the check and the record supporting the claimed remedy completion. Ask the dealer to clarify any mismatch between that record and the current vehicle-specific result.

Keep the reply beside your original search. A recent date on a screenshot is not, by itself, a record of when work was performed. You want to understand what each document actually establishes.

If a campaign is still open, obtain the manufacturer's current instructions and ask the dealer about the next step. Follow the official interim guidance for that campaign. This guide does not replace it.

## Keep service programmes and inspections separate

A manufacturer may have a programme outside a safety-recall search. Ask the dealer which system covers it and whether the actual vehicle is eligible. Do not rename every service programme a safety recall.

An independent inspection answers another set of questions about the candidate. A recall search and an inspection can both be useful while establishing different things. Bring unresolved record questions to the inspector; do not assume a successful search answers every inspection concern.

## Leave a clear handover note

A practical note has four parts: the search and date, the question about applicability, the supporting dealer or manufacturer record, and what remains unresolved. That is more useful at a viewing than a generic “recalls checked” tick.
