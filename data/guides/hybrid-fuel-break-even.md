A seller says the hybrid will save enough fuel to justify its price. You do not need to accept or dismiss that claim immediately. Turn it into a calculation with a boundary you can explain.

The tool above compares two gasoline-fuelled candidates using the same annual distance and fuel price. It works for a conventional gasoline car and a non-plug-in hybrid. It does not model an EV or a plug-in hybrid's mixture of electricity and gasoline.

## Put the candidates on the same basis

Find the fuel-economy entries for the exact year, powertrain and drivetrain using [FuelEconomy.gov](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/feg/Find.do?action=sbsSelect). Use US MPG throughout this example. A figure in imperial MPG belongs to a different unit system.

Write down how far you expect to drive and the price you intend to pay per US gallon. These are your assumptions. The [official cost-method notes](https://www.fueleconomy.gov/m/help.shtml) explain the defaults used by that service; they are not a promise about your future driving.

The tool calculates each annual fuel bill as distance divided by MPG, multiplied by the fuel price. It subtracts candidate B's fuel bill from candidate A's. A positive difference means B uses less fuel money under these entries.

## Recovering a premium is a separate calculation

For an illustrative comparison, use 12,000 miles per year, $3.50 per US gallon, 30 US MPG for A and 40 US MPG for B. The annual fuel scenarios are $1,400 and $1,050. B's fuel saving is $350 per year.

If B costs $2,000 more upfront, $2,000 divided by $350 gives about 5.7 years of fuel savings to recover that premium. These are fictional inputs. They do not describe a particular advertised car.

An extra price of zero needs no positive premium recovery. A negative extra price means B is cheaper upfront under your entries. If B has no annual fuel saving, a positive premium has no fuel-only payback. The tool states those cases directly rather than returning a misleading zero-year answer.

## Check the holding period you actually expect

A 5.7-year payback tells a different story if you plan to keep the car for three years rather than eight. Re-run the calculation with a lower annual distance. Then try a different fuel price. You are testing the dependence of the answer on assumptions, not predicting future fuel prices.

The calculation holds annual distance, MPG and price constant. It excludes financing, resale differences, insurance, maintenance, repairs and taxes. Those exclusions can change an ownership comparison even when the fuel arithmetic is correct.

## Return to the actual car

A candidate still needs the right identity, documented history and an independent inspection. An appealing fuel result cannot answer a question about this vehicle's condition.
