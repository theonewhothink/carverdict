A fresh-looking set of tires makes a used car feel cared for. Keep that observation. Then give the tires a separate place in the conversation with your inspector.

There is more to establish than whether the tread looks deep in the advert. You want to know what is fitted to this car, how its age and condition were assessed and whether the vehicle manufacturer's requirements were checked.

## Use the car's own reference

NHTSA directs drivers to the owner's manual or the vehicle's tire-information label for the recommended tire size and cold inflation pressure. The number on a tire's sidewall is not the vehicle's recommended inflation pressure.

For a viewing, our suggestion is to ask the inspector to identify the fitted tires and compare them with the requirements for the actual vehicle. If the wheels have been changed, mention that too. Do not choose a pressure or replacement specification from a photograph of another car with the same badge.

## Ask about age as well as appearance

NHTSA explains that appearance alone cannot reveal tire aging. The last four digits of the DOT identification number encode the production week and year. Manufacturer replacement guidance and a professional assessment remain relevant; a date code is not a certificate of safe remaining life.

Ask how the inspector assessed the tires, including anything they could not inspect. An invoice can help document a purchase or fitting date, but that date is a different fact from the manufacturing date and present condition.

## Make the next step specific

“Are the tires good?” invites a short answer. Try asking: “What is fitted, what did you inspect and is any further assessment or replacement recommended?” Let the professional explain the basis of the recommendation.

If work is advised, request a quote for the appropriate specification. Put that actual quote in your notes rather than assigning a generic tire allowance to every car. Check what is included so you can compare two quotes on the same basis.

A mismatch, damage or uncertainty deserves clarification before you treat a car as ready to use. This guide cannot tell you whether a particular vehicle is safe to drive.

## Keep the appeal and the evidence together

Good photographs are part of enjoying the car. They help you notice a design, a wheel style or a detail you want to ask about. They do not replace the closer assessment. Read [what a photograph cannot establish](/guides/what-car-photos-cannot-tell-you/) before using catalogue images to identify your candidate.

Take these questions into the [viewing plan](/guides/before-a-used-car-viewing/) and bring the answers into your [inspection report review](/guides/inspection-report-next-step/). That creates a record you can return to when the excitement of the viewing has passed.
