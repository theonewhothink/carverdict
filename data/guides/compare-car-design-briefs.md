Open photographs of the Miura, the original NSX and the McLaren F1. You can choose the shape you like immediately. A second question makes the comparison more rewarding: which engineering idea would you like to understand?

The table above gives you one selected archive fact for each project and a question to carry into the photographs. It compares ideas. It does not measure performance or assess any individual car for sale.

## Keep the fact and your observation in different columns

Lamborghini describes the Miura's transverse mid-mounted V12. Honda describes the original NSX's all-aluminium monocoque. McLaren describes the road-going F1's carbon-fibre monocoque. These facts come from the manufacturers' own archives, checked on the date below.

You might notice cabin placement, openings in the body or the relationship between the front and rear volumes. Write those as your observations. They are useful prompts for research, even when the photograph cannot tell you the answer.

For instance, an opening can make you wonder how air moves through a design. Its appearance alone does not establish a measured cooling result. Similarly, an exterior photograph does not identify every material used beneath the painted surface.

## Choose a comparison question with room for different answers

Instead of asking which car has the “best engineering,” ask how each project's selected construction or packaging choice relates to the shape you see. The archive can then explain a decision, while the photographs give you a way to notice it.

The table deliberately retains three different facts rather than pretending they are three values on one numerical scale. A transverse engine layout and a body construction material are different kinds of information. They can guide a discussion without being added into a winner's score.

## Check that the image belongs to the scope

Keep the original NSX separate from later cars carrying the name. Keep the F1 road car separate from the F1 GTR's racing story. For the Miura, verify which derivative the source or photograph discusses before borrowing a detail.

MotorJury's catalogue photographs can be references with uncertain build years or trims. Read the image note and attribution; do not use a convenient photograph to claim the exact specification of another example.

The [timeline guide](/guides/car-design-timeline/) helps you separate an introduction from later milestones. The [photo-evidence guide](/guides/what-car-photos-cannot-tell-you/) applies the same discipline when a vehicle is being advertised for sale.

## Save a reason as well as a name

Finish your comparison with one short note for each car. “I want to understand the engine packaging” gives you a next question. “I want to read about the body structure” gives you another. Neither requires you to pretend you have driven the car.
