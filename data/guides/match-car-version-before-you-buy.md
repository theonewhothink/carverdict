The badge on the boot looks right. The seller calls it a 2020 model. A guide you found discusses a hybrid. Those details may belong together, but that connection still needs evidence.

Start a small identity sheet before applying model-specific information. The aim is to keep an attractive nameplate from standing in for the exact vehicle you are considering.

## Keep the advert as a claim to check

Copy the seller's description into your own notes, along with the source and date. Keep the claimed model year, powertrain, drivetrain and market separate. Do not silently fill a gap from a photograph of a similar car.

Ask the seller which documents support those entries. Compare them with the labels and documents available for the actual vehicle. When two sources disagree, write down the disagreement and ask for clarification before using a version-specific guide.

## Use the decoder for the information it provides

[NHTSA's vPIC decoder](https://vpic.nhtsa.dot.gov/decoder/) identifies information encoded in a VIN using manufacturer-reported data. The service directs further questions about that information to the manufacturer. A decoded identity is useful evidence for matching records; it is not an inspection or a condition assessment.

Keep your actual VIN in the official service or in your private documents. You do not need to put it into a MotorJury chat, shared link or public comment.

If a needed field is missing or unclear, keep it unresolved. Ask the manufacturer or dealer for a vehicle-specific record instead of treating a blank field as confirmation of the advertised equipment.

## Match the scope of the source

A guide has its own boundaries: model year, market and version. The [MotorJury RAV4 brief](/buying-brief/) covers the stated 2019–2020 US gasoline and Hybrid versions. A photograph or similar badge cannot extend that research to another market or a plug-in version.

[NHTSA's recall search](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) distinguishes general year/make/model results from searches for a specific vehicle. Use the [recall-to-record workflow](/guides/recall-check-to-dealer-record/) when moving from a model-level concern to questions about applicability and completed work.

## Give the inspector the unresolved points

Send the identity sheet to the person inspecting the candidate. Separate what you have confirmed from the seller's claims and your remaining questions. That makes an uncertain detail visible without asking the inspector to guess what mattered to you.

For example, a listing may omit a drivetrain while an economy comparison depends on it. The useful next step is to confirm the drivetrain and select the corresponding comparison entry. Copying the nearest available MPG figure would hide the very uncertainty you are trying to resolve.

## Keep the evidence beside the conclusion

A useful note says which document or service supported each field and when you checked it. It also leaves room for corrections. Avoid a single green tick labelled “verified car” when several individual details remain unanswered.

Use the identity sheet before your [viewing questions](/guides/before-a-used-car-viewing/) and retain it when reading an [inspection report](/guides/inspection-report-next-step/).
