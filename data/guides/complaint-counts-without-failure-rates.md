A page shows a complaint total next to a model year. Another year has a smaller number. It is easy to read the difference as a league table: more reports, worse car.

That conclusion asks the total to answer questions it was not designed to answer. Reports can point you toward concerns worth investigating. The number alone does not tell you your chance of a failure, the condition of a car for sale or which version each concern belongs to.

## The missing denominator matters

To interpret a rate, you need an appropriate numerator and denominator for the same population and period. A raw complaint total supplies neither a verified count of all failures nor a complete measure of vehicles and use at risk.

Imagine two model years with the same hypothetical report total. They may have different numbers of vehicles on the road, ages, mileage and reporting behaviour. Without resolving those differences, dividing by a convenient production figure gives a precise-looking result rather than a defensible reliability measure.

That is why MotorJury does not turn these totals into a reliability percentage or an automatic BUY/AVOID label.

## Check the identity before reading the story

A model-level response may not distinguish every engine, trim, powertrain or market. A concern reported under a familiar nameplate is not automatically a concern documented for your exact vehicle.

Start with the year, make, model and market shown beside the record. Read the source-check date and matching status. If the service could not confirm a match, “not verified” is the useful answer. It does not mean zero.

A source that returned no matching reports at the recorded check is also narrower than a claim that no car has had a problem. Preserve that distinction when you talk to a seller or inspector.

## Read the concern, then decide what to ask

The report's description can suggest a question about symptoms, records or work performed. It is not an inspection finding about the vehicle in front of you. Component categories can overlap, so adding them together can also misrepresent the total.

Try a note with three lines: what the source describes, which version it appears to concern, and what evidence would clarify your candidate. For an actual purchase, take the note to a qualified inspector rather than diagnosing a fault from a count.

Our [2020 Lexus RX 350 record](/cars/lexus/rx-350/2020/) is an example of the scope labels and source checks to read. Its dated totals may change when sources refresh; this article deliberately does not freeze a number into a buying verdict.

## Keep recalls in another column

Complaint reports and recall campaigns are different records. A campaign count does not establish VIN applicability or completed remedies. For those questions, check the actual vehicle through the official service and obtain the relevant manufacturer/dealer evidence.

The practical result is a better question list, not a winner chosen by the biggest or smallest total. Compare condition, version, service evidence and your own costs before deciding. [Recalls, history and inspections](/guides/recalls-history-and-inspections/) shows how the checks fit together.
