Tesla's cars are the most complained-about electric cars in the federal record, and the least complained-about in the way that usually matters. Almost nothing in a Tesla's record is a powertrain failure. A large share of it is the driver-assistance system doing something the owner did not expect, and the rest is the kind of thing — screens, door handles, panel gaps — that a petrol-car owner would file under annoyance rather than reliability. Reading a Tesla page on this site therefore needs a different eye from reading a Ford page.

## Model S: 2012 to 2016

The early Model S is the one Tesla with a genuinely mechanical record. The drive units on 2012 to 2014 cars developed a whine and, in a proportion of cars, failed, and Tesla replaced them under warranty at a rate high enough to be reported by owners' surveys and to lead to an extended eight-year drive-unit warranty. The retractable door handles fail to present or to open. The touchscreen's embedded memory chip, which logs data continuously, wears out after several years and takes the screen, the reversing camera and the climate controls with it; the regulator's investigation ended in a 2021 recall of 2012 to 2018 Model S and 2016 to 2018 Model X for the chip, and Tesla replaced the computer under it. The front suspension's fore links were the subject of a 2021 investigation and a recall in China. The 2013 Model S carries the highest count of the early years. Battery degradation on these cars is modest — most have lost between ten and fifteen per cent of range after a decade — but a failed battery pack out of warranty is a five-figure repair, which is why MotorJury's Tesla pages carry battery-replacement cost ranges.

## Model 3: 2018 onward

The Model 3's launch year has the highest complaint count of the nameplate: 2018 cars were delivered with paint defects, misaligned panels, a touchscreen that rebooted, and, once the Autopilot software matured enough to use, phantom braking — the car braking hard on an open road for an obstacle that was not there. The phantom-braking complaints grew through 2021 and 2022 as Tesla removed radar from the sensor suite and relied on cameras alone, and the regulator opened an investigation. The heat pump fitted from 2021 failed in cold weather on a proportion of cars and was recalled. The mechanical record is small: the drive units are reliable, the battery packs have shown low degradation, and the brakes, which the regenerative system rarely uses, can seize from disuse in wet climates.

## What the record cannot tell you

The federal complaint database counts filings, and a Tesla owner is more likely to file one than a Camry owner: the cars attract attention, the software changes under the owner's feet, and the driver-assistance system generates events that feel like defects. So Tesla's complaint counts overstate its mechanical unreliability relative to a conventional car. In the other direction, the record undercounts one thing that matters: the cost of a repair when something does happen. Body repairs on aluminium structures, a replacement touchscreen, or a battery pack outside the eight-year warranty are expensive in a way the complaint count does not show, and insurance on a Tesla reflects it.

## What to check on a used Tesla

The battery warranty: eight years and between 100,000 and 150,000 miles depending on model, transferable, with a guarantee of seventy per cent capacity retention. Confirm the in-service date, because the eight years run from the first delivery. The screen: on a 2012 to 2018 Model S or 2016 to 2018 Model X, confirm the memory-chip recall was performed. The drive unit on an early Model S: listen for a whine that rises with speed, and check the warranty history. The paperwork: whether the car has Full Self-Driving or Enhanced Autopilot purchased, because Tesla has at times removed those features on resale. And the door handles on a Model S: press each one and watch it present.

## Summary

Buy: Model 3 2020 onward, Model S 2017 onward with the screen recall done. Acceptable: Model S 2015 to 2016 with drive-unit history. Watch: any Tesla's driver-assistance behaviour on the test drive, because that is where most of the record comes from.
